# New Jersey Overlay for Contract Review

Apply these points on top of the general checklist when the client is a New Jersey business, the contract is governed by NJ law, or the counterparty or performance is in NJ. **Treat every cite as `[VERIFY CITE]`** — the list is a review aid compiled 2026-11-02, not research. Confirm current law, especially where legislation is pending.

## Formation & enforceability
- **Governing law / forum.** NJ courts generally honor a chosen law under the Restatement (Second) Conflict of Laws § 187 unless it offends fundamental NJ policy. For an NJ client, prefer NJ law and venue in the client's county (Superior Court) or the District of New Jersey.
- **Statute of frauds.** N.J.S.A. 25:1-5 (certain agreements must be in writing), 25:1-13 (real estate), 12A:2-201 (sale of goods ≥ $500).
- **Covenant of good faith and fair dealing** is implied in every NJ contract (Sons of Thunder v. Borden). Clauses giving one side unfettered discretion are read against it.
- **Limitation of liability / exculpatory clauses** are enforceable between sophisticated commercial parties but construed strictly. Clauses excusing gross negligence, willful misconduct, or statutory violations are vulnerable.
- **Liquidated damages** must be a reasonable forecast and not a penalty (Wasserman's Inc. v. Middletown).

## Dispute resolution
- **Arbitration clauses must clearly waive the right to sue in court.** *Atalese v. U.S. Legal Services Group* (2014) requires the clause to explain, in some fashion, that the party is giving up the right to bring claims in court or to have a jury decide them. Applied to employment in *Flanzman v. Jenny Craig* (2020) and *Skuse v. Pfizer* (2020). The FAA preemption arguments are still being litigated. **If a contract's arbitration clause lacks express court/jury-waiver language, flag it High** (if the client wants arbitration) or note the defense (if the client wants to avoid arbitration).
- Jury-waiver clauses should be conspicuous and mutual.
- Fee-shifting is enforceable by contract (NJ follows the American Rule otherwise). Check for one-way fee provisions.

## Restrictive covenants & employment
- **Non-competes:** NJ has no statutory ban. Courts apply the common-law reasonableness test (*Solari Industries v. Malady*, 1970; *Whitmyer Bros. v. Doyle*, 1971): protects a legitimate interest, no undue hardship on the employee, not injurious to the public. Courts may "blue pencil" (partially enforce). **Pending legislation** (S1407 / A1829, introduced Jan. 2026) would sharply restrict non-competes. Check status before advising.
- Non-competes in a **sale of a business** get more latitude than employee non-competes.
- **NDAs and LAD claims:** N.J.S.A. 10:5-12.8 (2019) makes provisions in employment contracts and settlement agreements unenforceable against a current or former employee to the extent they conceal details of claims of discrimination, retaliation, or harassment, and requires a specific notice in settlements. Flag any such clause in employment or separation agreements.
- **Prospective waivers** of LAD procedural and substantive rights in employment contracts are unenforceable (N.J.S.A. 10:5-12.7).
- **Independent contractor classification:** NJ uses the "ABC" test for wage and unemployment law (*Hargrove v. Sleepy's*, 2015; N.J.S.A. 43:21-19(i)(6)), with stiff misclassification penalties since 2019–2021. Flag contractor agreements that look like employment.
- **CEPA** (whistleblower) rights can't be waived by contract.
- **NJ Trade Secrets Act**, N.J.S.A. 56:15-1 et seq. — align the NDA's definitions and remedies.

## Consumer-facing contracts (B2C)
- **Consumer Fraud Act** (N.J.S.A. 56:8-1 et seq.) — treble damages and mandatory attorney's fees, so it is a major exposure point for any business selling to consumers.
- **TCCWNA** (N.J.S.A. 56:12-14 to -18) — a consumer contract, notice, or sign can't include a provision violating clearly established legal rights, and can't say a provision may be void "in some jurisdictions" without saying whether it is void in NJ. Watch for generic "where prohibited by law" boilerplate in consumer terms.
- **Plain Language Act** (N.J.S.A. 56:12-1 et seq.) — consumer contracts must be written in simple, clear, understandable language.
- Auto-renewal and cancellation terms in consumer subscriptions: check current NJ law and the FTC rules.

## Business sales & entity matters
- **Bulk sale notice (N.J.S.A. 54:50-38).** The purchaser must file Form C-9600 with the Division of Taxation, received at least 10 business days before closing, for any transfer of business assets outside the ordinary course. Otherwise the purchaser can become liable for the seller's NJ taxes. The APA should:
  - make filing a closing condition
  - require the seller to cooperate
  - cover any Division-required escrow, which can exceed the purchase price
  - provide for release only on a clearance letter
  - include a seller indemnity for state taxes
- Equity sales by individuals are generally outside the bulk-sale notice; interests held by entities may not be. Confirm with the Division's FAQ.
- **LLCs:** NJ Revised Uniform LLC Act (N.J.S.A. 42:2C-1 et seq.). The operating agreement may modify but not eliminate certain duties. Check transfer restrictions, dissociation, and buyout provisions.
- **Corporations:** NJ Business Corporation Act (N.J.S.A. 14A). Shareholder written consent is unanimous unless the certificate permits less. Confirm when drafting consents.
- Check **entity good standing** (NJ annual reports) and the **Business Registration Certificate** where public contracts are involved.
- Transfer of NJ licenses and permits, and municipal approvals (e.g., certificate of occupancy, liquor license transfer), are frequent closing conditions.

## Commercial relationships
- **Franchise Practices Act** (N.J.S.A. 56:10-1 et seq.) — if the relationship could be a "franchise" (licensed mark + community of interest + NJ place of business), termination or non-renewal needs **good cause and 60 days' written notice**, and the protections can't be waived. Flag distribution and dealer agreements that may be de facto franchises.
- **Sales Representatives' Rights Act** (N.J.S.A. 2A:61A-1 et seq.) — commissioned sales rep agreements must be in writing and address commissions after termination; unpaid commissions carry enhanced damages.
- **Construction:** Prompt Payment Act (N.J.S.A. 2A:30A-1 et seq.) payment timelines; Construction Lien Law (N.J.S.A. 2A:44A) — commercial lien claim within 90 days of last work; lien waivers in advance are void.
- **Commercial leases:** NJ residential tenant protections (Anti-Eviction Act) don't apply, so negotiate everything. Watch personal guaranties, confessions of judgment (disfavored in NJ and subject to strict procedural requirements, R. 4:45-2 — confirm before relying), and environmental (ISRA, N.J.S.A. 13:1K-6) triggers on industrial establishments.

## Data, privacy & healthcare
- **NJ Data Privacy Act** (N.J.S.A. 56:8-166.4 et seq., effective Jan. 15, 2025) — controller/processor contract terms are required for covered businesses. Check the DPA terms.
- NJ data breach notification law (N.J.S.A. 56:8-161 et seq.) — align vendor breach-notice timelines.
- HIPAA business associate agreements where PHI is involved, plus any NJ health-information overlays.

## Lending & payment terms
- **Usury:** the NJ civil usury cap applies to individuals and some entities. Corporations and many LLCs can't raise usury as a civil defense, but criminal usury limits still apply (N.J.S.A. 2C:21-19). Confirm the borrower type and rate.
- Late fees and default interest must be reasonable, not penalties.
- Confessions of judgment / cognovit clauses: disfavored and procedurally restricted in NJ (R. 4:45-2); check enforceability before relying on them.
