## Superior Court captions and required filings
Caption format (Law Division example):

```
[ATTORNEY NAME], Esq. (NJ Attorney ID No. [ ])
[FIRM NAME]
[Address] | [Phone] | [Email]
Attorneys for Plaintiff [CLIENT]

[PLAINTIFF],                         SUPERIOR COURT OF NEW JERSEY
              Plaintiff,             LAW DIVISION: [COUNTY] COUNTY
   v.                                DOCKET NO. [ ]
[DEFENDANT],                                  Civil Action
              Defendant.             [DOCUMENT TITLE]
```

- Chancery: "CHANCERY DIVISION: GENERAL EQUITY PART, [COUNTY] COUNTY". Special Civil Part: "LAW DIVISION, SPECIAL CIVIL PART, [COUNTY] COUNTY".
- The first pleading needs: **Case Information Statement** (R. 4:5-1(b)(1)); **certification that the matter is not the subject of any other action** and identifying non-parties who should be joined (R. 4:5-1(b)(2)); **R. 1:38-7 certification** that confidential personal identifiers are redacted; **designation of trial counsel** (R. 4:25-4); **jury demand** if wanted.
- Most filings require a **certification in lieu of oath** (R. 1:4-4(b)) rather than a notarized affidavit.
- Motions: notice of motion with return date; proposed form of order (R. 1:6-2, R. 4:42-1); brief or letter brief; certification of service. Check the page limits and the requirement to request oral argument.
- Orders to show cause for emergent relief: verified complaint or certification plus a brief (R. 4:52).
- Federal (D.N.J.): follow the L.Civ.R. formats; motions use the court's motion-day calendar.

## Ethics rules that shape letters and agreements
- **RPC 3.4(g):** don't threaten criminal, administrative, or disciplinary charges to obtain an advantage in a civil matter. Keep demand letters to civil remedies.
- **RPC 4.2:** address represented parties through their counsel.
- **RPC 4.1:** no false statements of material fact to others.
- **RPC 1.5(b):** a new client gets fee terms in writing. **RPC 1.5(c) / R. 1:21-7:** contingent fees in writing.
- **RPC 1.13:** when representing an entity, say so, and say that its owners and officers are not individually clients unless agreed.
- **RPC 1.7:** a joint representation (e.g., the company and its founders, or buyer co-owners) needs informed written consent and a conflict-waiver paragraph.
- **R. 1:20A-6 pre-action notice:** before suing a client for fees, send notice by certified **and** regular mail (or hand delivery) including the district fee committee secretary's contact information, the client's right to request fee arbitration, and the warning that the right is lost if the client doesn't file within 30 days. Wait 30 days. The complaint must allege the notice was given.
- Advertising and solicitation rules (RPC 7.1–7.3) apply to client newsletters and outreach materials. Avoid unverifiable comparisons and guarantees of results.

## Entity documents (LLCs and corporations)
- **LLC (N.J.S.A. 42:2C, Revised Uniform LLC Act):** the operating agreement controls most matters. Action by written consent follows the operating agreement and the Act — confirm the section. Recite member-managed vs. manager-managed status. Include buy-sell provisions, transfer restrictions, dissociation, deadlock, and tax distributions.
- **Corporation (N.J.S.A. 14A):** board action by unanimous written consent (confirm N.J.S.A. 14A:6-7.1). Shareholder action without a meeting is unanimous unless the certificate of incorporation permits less, with notice to non-consenting shareholders (confirm N.J.S.A. 14A:5-6).
- Formation and amendment certificates are filed online with the **Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services**. Draft the information and the internal resolutions, not a paper form.
- Recite the entity exactly as registered (e.g., "Acme Supply, LLC, a New Jersey limited liability company").

## Transactional NJ add-ons
- **Asset purchase agreement:** bulk-sale clause covering:
  - purchaser files Form C-9600, received ≥10 business days before closing
  - seller cooperates and provides NJ tax IDs
  - escrow of any amount the Division requires, released on the clearance letter
  - seller indemnity for pre-closing NJ taxes
  - closing condition tied to the notice
- **Arbitration clause:** must state clearly that the parties waive the right to sue in court and to a jury trial (*Atalese*). Sample core language: "By agreeing to arbitration, each party waives its right to bring claims in court and to have a judge or jury decide them."
- **Governing law / venue:** New Jersey law; Superior Court, [County] County, or the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey.
- **Non-competes:** tailor scope, duration, and geography to the legitimate interest (NJ reasonableness test). Seller non-competes in a sale of a business can be broader than employee covenants. Note any pending NJ non-compete legislation.
- **Consumer-facing terms:** Plain Language Act and TCCWNA. Avoid "void where prohibited" boilerplate unless it specifies NJ.

## Employment and settlement documents
- Settlement and separation agreements involving discrimination, retaliation, or harassment claims: confidentiality provisions that conceal claim details are unenforceable against the employee, and NJ requires a specific notice in the agreement (N.J.S.A. 10:5-12.8). Draft accordingly and mark it for attorney confirmation of current requirements.
- No prospective waiver of LAD rights in employment contracts (N.J.S.A. 10:5-12.7).
- Contractor agreements: the ABC test governs classification. Don't rely on labels alone.
- ADEA/OWBPA 21/45-day review and 7-day revocation periods for releases from workers 40 and over (federal).

## Client-education handouts
A short plain-English handout for business clients (e.g., "Asset vs. Equity Purchase", "Preparing for Closing", "NJ Bulk-Sale Notice"). Structure it like this:
- a title that names the decision
- a 3–5 sentence "what this means for you"
- a few short sections or Q&As
- a "what to do next" checklist
- a footer: "This summary is general information, not legal advice for your situation. Contact [FIRM] before acting." Add the firm's name exactly as it appears on its letterhead.

Keep it to 1–2 pages and aim for about an 8th–10th grade reading level. Where outside resources are listed (consultants, agencies), state that they are listed as possible resources, not endorsements.
