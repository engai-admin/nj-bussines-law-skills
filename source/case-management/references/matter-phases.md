# Typical Matter Phases — NJ Business Law

Use these phases as the starting point for a task plan, then tailor them to the matter. Deadlines that come from rules go to the deadline-tracker skill (with the NJ profile).

## Contents
- Purchase or sale of a business
- Entity formation
- Owner / governance documents and buyouts
- Business litigation in NJ Superior Court
- LLC / partner / shareholder dispute (Chancery)
- Collections
- Contract negotiation (standalone)
- Closing out a matter

---

## Purchase or sale of a business (NJ)
1. **Intake.** Conflicts; engagement letter (who is the client — entity, owners, or both); assemble the deal team (CPA, broker, lender, insurance).
2. **Structure & LOI.** Asset vs. equity purchase; price and adjustments; exclusivity; confidentiality. Flag bulk-sale exposure early when it is an asset deal.
3. **Due diligence.** Cover these areas:
   - NJ entity status and annual reports (Division of Revenue)
   - UCC, judgment, and tax lien searches; litigation searches
   - material contracts, change-of-control and assignment clauses
   - leases
   - permits and licenses, municipal approvals, liquor license if any
   - employees and benefits, contractor classification (ABC test)
   - NJ and federal taxes
   - environmental: does ISRA apply?
   - IP
   - HIPAA and other regulatory compliance where relevant
4. **Drafting & negotiation.** Prepare the purchase agreement, disclosure schedules, bill of sale, assignment and assumption, lease assignment or new lease, seller non-compete, consulting and employment agreements, and escrow agreement.
5. **Pre-closing.** File the **bulk-sale notice (Form C-9600)** so the Division of Taxation *receives* it at least 10 business days before closing. Track the Division's response (escrow, returns-required, or clearance letter). Also handle third-party consents, permit and license transfers, and financing conditions.
6. **Closing.** Work the closing checklist and funds flow (including any Division-required escrow), signature pages, and filings.
7. **Post-closing.**
   - escrow release on the Division's clearance letter
   - working-capital true-up
   - earn-out statements
   - survival-period and holdback release dates
   - new-owner registrations

## Entity formation (NJ)
1. Intake and choice of entity: LLC vs. corporation (S-election is the CPA's call).
2. Name availability; certificate of formation or incorporation (Division of Revenue); registered agent.
3. EIN; NJ-REG tax and employer registration.
4. Operating agreement or bylaws and shareholder agreement; initial resolutions or consents; membership or stock ledger.
5. Licenses and permits; Business Registration Certificate if the client will contract with public entities.
6. Compliance calendar: annual report by the end of the anniversary month, and an annual minutes/consent reminder (deadline-tracker).

## Owner / governance documents and buyouts
Intake (which owners are clients?) → review of the existing operating or shareholder agreement → term sheet with buy-sell triggers (death, disability, divorce, departure, deadlock) and a valuation method → draft and negotiate → execution → funding (insurance, notes) → ledger and filings.

## Business litigation — NJ Superior Court (Law Division / CBLP)
1. **Intake.** Conflicts; engagement; litigation hold letter; calendar the limitations period. Check for an arbitration clause and whether it is enforceable under *Atalese*.
2. **Pre-suit.** Demand letter (no threats of criminal charges, RPC 3.4(g)); insurance notice; damages workup; forum choice (Special Civil Part ≤ $20k / Law Division / CBLP ≥ $200k / D.N.J.).
3. **Pleadings.** File the complaint with:
   - Case Information Statement (CIS)
   - R. 4:5-1(b)(2) certification
   - R. 1:38-7 certification
   - designation of trial counsel
   - jury demand

   Serve through eCourts and by service of process. The answer is due 35 days after service (R. 4:6-1); R. 4:6-2 motions are the alternative.
4. **Discovery.** The track assignment sets the discovery end date. Interrogatory answers are due in 60 days and **can't be extended by consent**. Document responses are due in 35 days. **Requests for admission are deemed admitted after 30 days.** Depositions, experts, and extensions follow R. 4:24-1.
5. **Motions.** Return-date practice (16 / 8 / 4 days). Summary judgment runs 28 / 10 / 4 days and must be returnable at least 30 days before trial.
6. **ADR.** Mandatory arbitration for certain case types, plus mediation.
7. **Trial & post-trial.** Reconsideration and new-trial motions are due in 20 days; notice of appeal in 45 days.
8. **Closing.** Settlement agreement (watch the N.J.S.A. 10:5-12.8 limits on NDAs for employee claims); stipulation of dismissal; satisfaction of judgment.

## LLC / partner / shareholder dispute (Chancery, General Equity)
Intake (whose interests are aligned? are separate counsel needed?) → review of the governing documents (buyout, deadlock, dissociation provisions) → evaluate interim relief by order to show cause (R. 4:52) → verified complaint (oppression, accounting, dissolution, breach of fiduciary duty) → expedited discovery → valuation experts → resolution by buyout or judicial dissolution.

## Collections
Demand letter → confirm the debtor entity and its assets (UCC and judgment searches) → choose the forum by amount (Small Claims ≤ $5k, Special Civil Part ≤ $20k, Law Division) → complaint → default (R. 4:43) or judgment → post-judgment: docket the judgment, information subpoena, wage execution or bank levy.

## Contract negotiation (standalone)
Intake → review with the contract-review skill (NJ overlay) → client call on the issues → send markup → negotiation rounds, tracking versions → execution → put dated obligations (renewal windows, cure periods, notice deadlines) into the deadline log.

## Closing out a matter
- Final invoice and trust account reconciliation (return any unearned retainer)
- Closing letter confirming the representation has ended and listing any ongoing obligations or dates the client must track
- Return or retain the file under the firm's policy
- Add relationship follow-ups (check-ins, legal updates) to the separate client-care list
